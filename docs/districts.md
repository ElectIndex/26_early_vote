# Congressional districts — `output/districts/<st>.csv` and `output/counterfactual_districts.csv`

Two tables, added 2026-10-09. The first is the early-vote count by House
district; the second is the 2024 thought experiment by House district. Both
are built on one piece of reference data, and most of what a reader needs to
distrust them follows from how that data is used.

## The crosswalk — `data/crosswalk/county_cd_weights.csv`

One row per county × district segment, 3,760 rows over 3,142 counties and all
435 districts, generated by `scripts/build_cd_weights.py` from the House
forecast's `county_segments.csv` (the block-level assignments behind
electindex.com/forecasts). The maps are the ones in force for the **2026**
election — the 2025 redraws in Texas, California, Missouri, North Carolina,
Ohio and Utah included — which is also the map the site draws.

| column | meaning |
|---|---|
| `weight` | the share of the county's 2024 presidential vote cast inside this district; sums to exactly 1.000000 across the county |
| `votes_dem`, `votes_rep`, `votes_total` | the segment's own 2024 presidential vote (block-level sums, not certified returns) |
| `basis_note` | blank; would read `population` or `equal` if a county had no 2024 vote to weight by. None does. |

The District of Columbia is dropped (no House seat). A test pins the set of
district codes to the site's shipped district geometry and the set of counties
to `_fips`'s table for a sample of states, so a regenerated crosswalk that
drifts from either fails before it is committed. Regenerate with

    python3 scripts/build_cd_weights.py ~/ElectIndex/forecasts/usa/data/county_segments.csv

and commit the result in a change that says why.

## `output/districts/<st>.csv` — the count by district

Same shape as `counties/<st>.csv`, keyed by `cd_code` ("NC-01"; an at-large
seat is "-01", because that is how the geometry keys it), plus one column:

* **`basis = reported`** — the state's own file carries a district column and
  the adapter counted ballots into districts directly. Three today: North
  Carolina (`cong_dist_desc`), Virginia (`CongressionalDistrict`), Maryland
  (`congressional_district_code`). Pennsylvania's dataset has a `congressional`
  column too and is not yet read for it (it needs a second grouped SoQL query).
* **`basis = apportioned`** — split from the county file: each county's daily
  figures are divided across its segments by `weight` and summed per
  district. Largest-remainder rounding makes the district rows sum back to the
  county rows to the ballot.

Three rules decide which is which, all in `ev.districts.rebuild`:

1. **Reported beats apportioned**, per (cycle, day). A day the state labelled
   is never split; a day it did not label is.
2. **Only the current cycle may be reported.** A past cycle's labels were that
   cycle's districts, and NC-01 was redrawn for 2026, so 2022 and 2024 are
   always apportioned onto today's map and every "vs 2024" comparison is on
   one geography. In 2028 the 2026 rows fall out on the first rebuild for the
   same reason, with no adapter knowing about it.
3. **The blank rule survives the split, both ways.** A blank county field is
   blank in every district; a district fed by two counties, one reporting
   party and one not, has blank party — the sum over the one that reported is
   not the district's count, it is an unknown fraction of it.

The table is DERIVED and rebuilt whole (`replace=True`) after each state's
county file is written, by `ingest`, `backfill` and `python -m ev districts`.
Provenance on an apportioned row is the county rows' own — the split is ours,
the count is theirs.

What an apportioned district row is NOT: evidence of where a county's early
voters live. It assumes a county's early ballots come from its districts in
the same proportion as its 2024 vote, which is wrong in every county whose
districts turn out at different rates. The page prints the basis beside every
district figure for that reason.

## `output/counterfactual_districts.csv` — the thought experiment by district

A companion to `counterfactual.csv`, written by the same command from exactly
the rows it just wrote. A district gets a row for the state-days its state got
one and for no others, so every refusal in `docs/counterfactual.md` — no
like-for-like reference day, partial geography, stub reference curve — carries
through unchanged.

Where the state row weights counties by ballots and values each at its
certified 2024 margin, the district row weights **segments** by
`ballots × weight` and values each at the segment's own 2024 margin:

    composition_margin = Σ_s ballots_c(s)·w_s·margin_s / Σ_s ballots_c(s)·w_s

The identity holds (weight every segment by its own 2024 vote and you get the
district's 2024 margin — tested), and summed to a state the segments land
within a point of the certified two-party margin for every state checked.

Two limits a reader should know:

* **The split within a county is proportional**, so this cannot see a
  county's early voters coming disproportionately from one of its districts.
  What moves a district's reading is the mix of *counties* feeding it.
* **The band is the state's.** There is no district-level ground truth to
  score against, so a district row carries its state row's half-width,
  widened to contain zero on an early day exactly as the state's is. A
  district cannot be better measured than the state it sits in. `confidence`,
  `completeness` and `reference_completeness` are the state's too.

`counties_used` / `counties_total` say how many of a district's counties are
in; `ballots` and `reference_ballots` are the district's apportioned share of
the state's ballots on each side.

`output/counterfactual_districts_latest.csv` is the same table reduced to the
newest row per (cycle, state, district) — a few hundred KB where the full file
is the whole daily history — derived from the full file on every write so the
two cannot disagree. The page's district maps read it and fall back to the
full file.
